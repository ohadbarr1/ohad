# fox

## Register
product

## Users
Three audiences on one data engine, each through its own door (plan 01.11.26, "three doors, one site"). The choice lives in the reader's browser; `/` asks once.
- **Saver (חוסך).** An employee with a pension, a hishtalmut and maybe a gemel, usually on a phone, ten minutes at a time. Knows the product names, not the jargon. Wants to know: am I paying too much, is my track any good, what should I ask my agent.
- **Agent (סוכן).** A pension agent or adviser preparing for a client meeting on a desktop or tablet. Fluent in products and fees, not in IFRS 17. Wants a pension-first comparison, default-fund terms, managers' fees, and something to hand the client.
- **Pro.** The owner and analysts: a professional investor (CPA, alternatives and listed equities) covering Israeli insurers and long-term-savings managers. Works on a large monitor in long sessions, usually evenings, moving between a filing PDF, a spreadsheet and this tool. Fluent in IFRS 17; impatient with explanation.

## Product purpose
The best free long-term-savings data in Israel, net of fees and sourced to the regulator, made usable by each audience.
- **Saver:** a plain answer in shekels and words: what I have, what it costs me, where it stands against similar tracks. Descriptive, never "move to fund X".
- **Agent:** a pension-first track picker, comparison, default funds and managers' fees, ready for a client meeting.
- **Pro:** a research terminal: reported figures with a page citation, long history, peer comparison, valuation, scenarios, and the source documents one click away. Success is answering "what changed, versus whom, and what is it worth" faster than opening the PDFs. Every module built for Pro stays, at its address, in the Pro door.

## Brand personality
fox is named after Fawkes, the phoenix. Three words: precise, warm, alive. Investment-bank density with the confidence of a modern product (Linear, Fiscal.ai), not a corporate BI report.

## Anti-references
- Power BI / EY InsurTool look: stock-photo backgrounds, yellow slabs, single-quarter cross-sections.
- Generic "AI dashboard": navy and gold, hero metrics with gradient accents, glass cards, methodology prose.
- Bloomberg-terminal cosplay: neon on black, monospace everywhere.

## Design principles
1. A number, a chart, a source. In Pro, no explanatory prose in the UI. The saver's door speaks plain Hebrew sentences, and the agent's door uses short ones; the figures under them stay sourced either way.
2. Dense but ranked: one thing leads each screen; the rest recedes.
3. Hebrew first, RTL; digits and tickers stay LTR and tabular.
4. Motion marks a change of state or arrival of data, and the brand gets a few expressive moments. Never blocks reading; honours reduced motion.
5. Estimates, derived values and assumptions are always labelled as such.

## Visual direction
Scene: evening, dim room, 27-inch monitor, a filing open beside it. Dark first on warm ink (not blue-black), one ember accent taken from the phoenix; the ember-to-magenta gradient lives only in the brand mark and a few brand moments. A real light theme exists for daytime and print.
